Research

My field is computational chemistry and my method is machine learning. I build models that predict how molecules bind and how active they will be, generative models that design ligands, peptides and proteins, and agentic systems that put these models to work.

At Takeda I build and benchmark these models, the GPU pipelines that run them and the harnesses through which AI agents use them, with computational chemists, structural biologists and data scientists. The work is confidential, so this page describes directions rather than results. Beneath it is a wider interest in AI for science: learned models, physical simulation and AI agents working together.

DirectionsTakeda, since June 2025

Binding and activity

Predicting how tightly a ligand binds its target, the pose it binds in, and how far to trust that pose, so candidates can be ranked for hit triage and lead optimization before anything is synthesized.

Affinity and pose prediction, pose-quality assessment, ensemble consensus

Generative design

Generative models that propose ligands for a given pocket, and peptides and proteins with designed sequences, including noncanonical amino acids, checked against physics-based simulation.

Diffusion, transformer and other 3D generative models; pocket-conditioned generation

Binding sites

Where binding happens: pockets, including cryptic ones that open only in some conformations; ternary complexes for targeted protein degradation; and the contacts, waters and metal ions that hold a complex together.

Pocket and cryptic pocket detection, ternary complex modeling

Molecular co-folding

Predicting a protein and its ligand together, as one complex, instead of docking a molecule into a fixed structure. I build pipelines around co-folding models, from sequence alignments to the complexes they produce, assess the poses, and adapt the models to drug-discovery targets.

Co-folding and MSA-based structure prediction, fine-tuning, GPU pipelines

Agentic workflows

AI agents that carry out computational work: planning a study, calling structure-prediction, affinity and simulation tools, checking what comes back, and passing results on. I build the execution harnesses and orchestration that let several agents work in parallel on scientific software, and keep their runs reliable and reproducible.

Agent harnesses, multi-agent orchestration, tool integration, workflow automation

2024–2025

SuperWater: where water sits

A score-based diffusion model on E(3)-equivariant networks that places water molecules on protein structures, with a confidence model that filters and clusters them. It began as DiffWater, which took first place at Vanderbilt’s Spring 2024 AI Showcase.

Antigen surfaces flattened into images so that CNNs can predict antibody binding sites (AUC above 0.93), and a follow-up that predicts conformational B-cell epitopes with a protein language model.

Colophon

Set in Archivo, a grotesque with a variable width axis, on a twelve-column grid, with Noto Serif TC for Chinese. One accent, the blue of an electron-density map. The mark is an X beside a K drawn as a stereocentre: its stem is two bonds in the plane of the page, its arms a solid wedge and a hashed wedge; it is set out by tools/monogram.py. The landscape on the home page is invented; the sampler is ordinary annealed Langevin dynamics. Molecules are drawn from RDKit coordinates. Every animation shares one curve, a quick start and a long release. Press G to see the grid.
